💬 5 Useful Phrases — 實用句型
1. "I'm here on a working holiday visa."
我持打工度假簽證來這裡的。
🎯 Use at: Immigration counter when the officer asks your purpose of visit.
A: What is your purpose of visit?
B: I'm here on a working holiday visa. I plan to work and travel in Canada.
2. "Could you speak more slowly, please?"
可以請你說慢一點嗎？
🎯 Use: Anytime someone speaks too fast. Canadians will happily slow down.
A: YouneedtogodowntothesecondfloortofindtheCarouselB.
B: Sorry, could you speak more slowly, please?
3. "Where can I find...?"
我可以在哪裡找到……？
🎯 Use: Asking for locations at the airport, in a store, or anywhere new.
B: Excuse me, where can I find the SkyTrain station?
A: Go straight and follow the signs. It's on the lower level.
4. "I just arrived in Canada."
我剛到加拿大。
📖 Reference: Great conversation opener when meeting anyone new.
B: I just arrived in Canada yesterday. Everything feels so new!
A: Welcome! Where are you from?
5. "Nice to meet you. I'm [name] from Taiwan."
很高興認識你。我是來自台灣的[名字]。
📖 Reference: Standard self-introduction. Simple and friendly.
B: Hi! Nice to meet you. I'm Wei from Taiwan.
A: Nice to meet you too, Wei! Welcome to Vancouver!
🎭 5 Dialogue Practices — 對話練習
1. At Immigration — 入境審查
Required 必練
📍 You just landed at Vancouver International Airport. You are at the immigration counter with your passport, letter of introduction, and proof of funds.
Officer: Good afternoon. Passport, please.
You: Here you go.
Officer: What is the purpose of your visit to Canada?
You: I'm here on a working holiday visa. Here is my letter of introduction.
Officer: How long do you plan to stay?
You: About one year. My visa is valid until May 2028.
Officer: Do you have proof of funds?
You: Yes, here is my bank statement.
Officer: Do you have travel insurance?
You: Yes, it covers my entire stay.
Officer: Great. Welcome to Canada. Please go to the next counter to get your work permit.
You: Thank you very much!
💡 Teacher Note: Practice the full dialogue first, then cover the student's lines and let them respond naturally to the officer's questions.
2. At Customs — 海關申報
Required 必練
📍 You collected your luggage and are now going through customs. The officer checks your declaration card.
Officer: Do you have anything to declare?
You: Yes, I have some dried fruit and tea from Taiwan.
Officer: Can I see them, please?
You: Sure, they're in this bag.
Officer: These are fine. How much cash are you carrying?
You: About 3,000 Canadian dollars.
Officer: That's under the limit. You're good to go. Welcome to Canada.
You: Thank you! Have a good day.
💡 Teacher Note: After practice, create variations — what if the student has nothing to declare? What if an item is confiscated?
3. Meeting Someone New — 認識新朋友
Required 必練
📍 You just checked into your hostel. Another traveler starts talking to you in the common area.
Traveler: Hey! Did you just arrive today?
You: Yes, I just arrived this afternoon. I'm from Taiwan.
Traveler: Cool! I'm Sarah from Brazil. Nice to meet you!
You: Nice to meet you too! I'm Wei. Are you here on a working holiday too?
Traveler: Yeah! I've been here for three months. What do you do back home?
You: I work as a rehabilitation specialist. I also have experience as a lifeguard.
Traveler: Oh nice! There are lots of fitness jobs here. Are you nervous about living in Canada?
You: A little nervous, but also very excited. My English isn't perfect yet, but I'm working on it!
Traveler: Don't worry, everyone here is really friendly. You'll be fine!
💡 Teacher Note: This is the most important dialogue for building confidence. Let the student personalize the answers with their own real information.
4. Taking a Taxi from the Airport — 從機場搭計程車
Choice 選練 A
📍 You exit the airport and get into a taxi to go to your hostel in downtown Vancouver.
Driver: Where to?
You: Hi, can you take me to 123 Main Street, please?
Driver: Sure. That's downtown. It'll take about 30 minutes.
You: Sounds good. How much will it cost?
Driver: About 35 to 40 dollars, depending on traffic.
You: Okay, no problem. Can I pay by credit card?
Driver: Yes, we accept cards. Is this your first time in Vancouver?
You: Yes! I just arrived from Taiwan. I'm here on a working holiday.
Driver: Welcome! Vancouver is a great city. You'll love it.
💡 Teacher Note: Practice tipping etiquette — should you tip a taxi driver? (Yes, 10–15%)
5. Checking into a Hostel — 辦理入住
Choice 選練 B
📍 You arrive at your hostel and need to check in at the front desk.
Staff: Hi there! Welcome. Do you have a reservation?
You: Yes, I booked online. My name is Wei Chen.
Staff: Let me check... Yes, I found it. You're staying for five nights, correct?
You: That's right.
Staff: Can I see your passport, please?
You: Sure, here you go.
Staff: Great. Your room is on the second floor. The Wi-Fi password is on this card. Breakfast is from 7 to 9.
You: Thank you! Where can I do laundry?
Staff: The laundry room is in the basement. It's $2 per load.
You: Perfect. Thank you for your help!
💡 Teacher Note: After practice, ask the student to role-reverse — they play the staff and you play the guest.
🗣️ 5 Speaking Questions — 口說問題（3 級難度，學生選 3 題回答）
Level 1 · Closed 封閉式
1. Is this your first time living in another country?
Hint: Answer yes or no, then add 1 sentence to explain.
Level 1 · Closed 封閉式
2. Would you prefer to live in Vancouver or Toronto? Why?
Hint: Pick one city and give 1–2 reasons.
Level 2 · Short Answer 短答
3. Why did you choose Canada for your working holiday?
Hint: Talk about 2–3 reasons. Try to speak for at least 30 seconds.
Level 2 · Short Answer 短答
4. What are three things you want to do in your first week in Canada?
Hint: Use "First, I want to... Second, I plan to... Third, I hope to..."
Level 3 · Open 開放式
5. Imagine it's one year from now. You are back in Taiwan talking to a friend. Describe your life in Canada — what did you do, where did you work, and what did you learn?
Hint: This is a challenge! Try to speak for 1–2 minutes without stopping. Use past tense. It's okay to make mistakes.
🍁 2 Canadian Culture Topics
☀️ Warm-Up Culture (課堂開頭)
Tim Hortons — Canada's Coffee Culture
Tim Hortons (often called "Timmies") is the most famous coffee chain in Canada. There are over 5,000 locations across the country — you can find one on almost every street corner. It was founded in 1964 by a hockey player named Tim Horton.

The most popular order is a "double-double" — coffee with two creams and two sugars. Other popular items include Timbits (small donut holes), iced cappuccinos, and breakfast sandwiches.

For Canadians, Tim Hortons is more than just a coffee shop — it's a cultural symbol. Many people start their day with a Timmies run. Saying "Let's grab a Timmies" is a casual way to invite someone for coffee. It's also one of the cheapest coffee options in Canada, making it perfect for WHV budgets!
☕ Discussion Questions

1. Do you have a favourite coffee chain in Taiwan? How often do you drink coffee?
2. In Canada, people say "Let's grab a coffee" to mean "Let's hang out." Do people in Taiwan use food or drinks to invite friends to socialize?
3. Would you order a "double-double"? Why or why not?

🌙 End-of-Class Culture (課堂結尾)
The Canadian "Sorry" Culture
Canadians are famous for saying "sorry" — even when it's not their fault! If someone bumps into YOU on the street, they will often say "sorry." If you step on someone's foot, THEY might apologize first.

In 2009, the province of Ontario even passed the Apology Act, which says that saying "sorry" cannot be used as an admission of guilt in court. That's how much Canadians say sorry!

For newcomers from Taiwan, this might feel strange at first. In Canadian culture, "sorry" is not just an apology — it's a social lubricant. It means "I notice you" and "I respect your space." You'll start saying "sorry" constantly after a few weeks in Canada. It's contagious!
🇨🇦 Discussion Questions

1. How often do people say "sorry" in Taiwan? Is it similar or different from Canada?
2. Can you think of a situation where saying "sorry" is important even if it's not your fault?

🔊 Pronunciation Spotlight — /θ/ and /ð/ (the "th" sounds)

The "th" sound is one of the hardest sounds for Chinese speakers because it doesn't exist in Mandarin. There are two "th" sounds in English:

/θ/ — voiceless "th" (no vibration in your throat)

🗣️ How to make this sound: Put the tip of your tongue between your teeth. Blow air out gently. Your throat does NOT vibrate.

Words from this lesson:

think /θɪŋk/ NOT "sink"
three /θriː/ NOT "sree"
thank you /θæŋk juː/ NOT "sank you"

/ð/ — voiced "th" (your throat vibrates)

🗣️ How to make this sound: Same tongue position, but this time your throat vibrates. Touch your throat to feel it.

Words from this lesson:

this /ðɪs/ NOT "dis"
the /ðə/ NOT "da"
there /ðɛr/ NOT "dare"

🎯 Practice Exercise (3 minutes)

Read these sentences aloud. Focus on every "th" sound:

1. "Thank you for the information."
2. "I think this is the right terminal."
3. "Is there a washroom near the gate?"
4. "The weather in Canada is different than Taiwan."
5. "I'm thinking about three things I want to do this week."

💡 Teacher Tip: Have the student hold a small piece of paper in front of their mouth. When they say /θ/ correctly, the paper should move slightly from the air flow.
